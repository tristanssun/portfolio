Screams before silence.

While Girl with a Pearl Earring reinforces the Dutch Golden Age tenets of femininity and modesty, Girl with a Pierced Eardrum instead challenges cultural preconceptions that cities shelter those who live in them, elucidating how the modern urban centers characterized by surveillance, danger, anonymization and industry malign and silence individuals, urging viewers to recognize and resist that imposition.

01 SCREAMS

SCREAMS

In a forlorn Bristol dockyard, Johannes Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring stands abandoned, superimposed onto a grungy off-white wall. In place of the glowing pearl of soft white light on her earlobe, a bellicose alarm with sharp ridges protrudes aggressively. All is silent save for the shriek of the alarm, cutting through the cool night air. Framed by bricks and a spiked fence, it's unclear whether the builders were trying to keep people out… or to keep her in.

02 FEMININE ORIGINS

FEMININE
ORIGINS

Vermeer's initial painting, completed during the Dutch Golden Age, reinforces feminine standards: modesty, refinement, a sense of grace. The girl's direct gaze creates an egalitarian sensation, inviting viewers to admire her beauty. Her light, soft skin symbolizes purity; this was taken to an extreme in WWII, when the Girl with a Pearl Earring was hidden in a secret bunker in the Netherlands during the Nazi occupation. Outside, the war churned on, but underground she continued to smile knowingly, protected (like culture dictated that women should be, in patriarchies) from outside cruelty. Both the painting itself and the paintings' environment reinforced women as beautiful, graceful objects to be sheltered.

03 REBELLION

REBELLION

Banksy's Girl with a Pierced Eardrum transplants Vermeer's girl into a space so unbecoming that it changes the work's meaning completely. Every other aspect of the mural is cold, hard and industrial save for the wide eyes and slightly parted lips of the young girl's face. Her supple contours attract the eye, while the industrial flatness of everything else repels it; thus, the work underlines the person as out of place in an impersonal, industrial city world. Unlike her original egalitarian gaze, the girl in Banksy's mural looks down at us from her vantage on the wall, creating a sense of wariness in place of intimacy. The title, too, conveys how the urban city has maligned this girl. "Pierced" (mutilation) replaces "pearl" (adornment). Rather than being adorned by a beautiful pearl, her ear has been pierced by the scream of the bitter yellow alarm. Journalist Malcolm Rigby described the meaning of the Girl with a Pierced Eardrum as follows: "wherever you live you will hear alarms going off."

03 REBELLION (continued)

REBELLION

The alarm is a symbol of surveillance, danger and industry. Surveillance and danger, because fear of danger led to the installation of a surveillance device. Industry, because of its incongruous boxy and mass-producable shape next to the girl's organically curved face. Despite occupying only a small area, the alarm's contrast with the rest of the mural and the deep purple hues of Vermeer's original painting allow it to serve an outsize effect in shaping the mural's cultural work.

04 SILENCE

SILENCE

In 2020, the cultural work of the Girl with a Pierced Eardrum grew after a face mask was pasted over her delicately parted (as if she was trying to speak) lips. The mask represents how urban responses to the COVID-19 pandemic stripped away intimacy and human connection, silencing her and billions of others around the world.

05 CONSENT

CONSENT

In the summer of 2020, hundreds of thousands of Bristol citizens were required to wear face masks and social distance, raising debates about government power vs individual rights. Banksy's work represents how the government's heavy hand culture influences urban cities, stripping away self-determinism and individual rights before and during the pandemic. Unable to escape the city landscape, the girl endures the screams of the alarm; unable to remove her mask, the girl loses intimacy and human connection.

Some experts argue the face mask was added as an act of solidarity for frontline healthcare workers, not to criticize COVID-19 policies in urban centers. However, these two points are actually not mutually exclusive. While masks silence human connection, they also prevent the spread of COVID-19, and so in this case the government's imposition was carried out with good intentions. The negative effects of COVID-19 policies on human connection and intimacy remain significant even though those policies were instituted with good intentions. 05 CONSENT (continued)

CONSENT

Anonymization pervades the cultural work of both works. In Girl with a Pearl Earring, the girl's anonymity allows her to stand for all women, strengthening the painting's cultural work: reinforcing the feminine standards of Dutch Golden Age culture. However, in Girl with a Pierced Eardrum, true to the reality of millions of citydwellers, anonymization turns the city against the girl. Due to the suppression of human connection and intimacy, she becomes anonymous, and this anonymity maligns and silences her, lost in the riptide of millions of people. Lacking human connection because of the harsh cityscape, the girl's humanity is lost.

06 UNMASKED

UNMASKED

Banksy juxtaposes Johannes Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring against the worst parts of city life to allow his public mural, Girl with a Pierced Eardrum, to perform a completely different cultural work. While Vermeer's portrait reinforced Dutch Golden Age stereotypes of women as pure and beautiful, Banksy's mural highlights how modern city culture maligns even the purest individuals through surveillance, danger, anonymization, and industry.
